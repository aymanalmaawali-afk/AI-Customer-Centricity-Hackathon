Home Wi‑Fi Check

Find out why your internet is slow, see your Wi‑Fi coverage in AR and 3D, and fix it yourself — or hand a complete report to support. Everything runs in your phone’s browser; there’s no app to install.

Before you start

  • A smartphone with a recent browser (Chrome, Safari, Edge or Samsung Internet).
  • The mobile number linked to your broadband account.
  • Access to your router — you’ll point the camera at its lights and cables.
  • Wi‑Fi turned on, and your phone connected to your home network.
Good to know: the camera maps your rooms and spots obstacles, but it can’t measure Wi‑Fi. Signal and speed come from your router, our network and your phone. Every number in the app is labelled so you know where it came from.

How to read the labels

LabelMeaning
MEASUREDA real reading from your router, a line test or your phone.
ESTIMATEDCalculated from measured points — coverage maps and before/after predictions.
AISomething the AI detected (an object on camera) or concluded (a likely cause).

Step by step

The app walks you through each step. You can go back at any time.

1 · Choose your language

Tap English or العربية. Arabic reads right to left throughout. Your choice applies everywhere — screens, the Ava assistant, voice, AR guidance and reports. To switch later, tap the language button at the top of the start screen.

2 · Choose what to scan

Scan a room checks Wi‑Fi coverage and dead zones. Scan a device takes you straight to a camera-guided check of your router’s lights and cables. You can switch between the two inside the camera view with the Room / Device toggle.

3 · Find your service

Enter the mobile number on your broadband account and tap Find my service. Check the details shown — plan, router, street cabinet and any faults in your area — then tap Continue. If it isn’t you, tap Not me and try another number.

4 · Allow the camera

Tap Allow camera and accept your browser’s prompt. Video is processed on your phone and never saved. If you’d rather not, choose Use a demo room instead.

5 · Scan each room

Walk slowly and sweep the walls, then the floor. Boxes appear around things that affect Wi‑Fi, coloured by impact. Your phone also records the signal in each room. When the bar is full, tap Next room; after the last room, tap Finish scan.

  • Red — high impact: metal, brick, cabinets around the router.
  • Yellow — medium: mirrors, wardrobes, microwaves.
  • Grey — low: sofas, plaster walls.

6 · Measure your connection

The app checks your router status, wired speed at the router, latency, packet loss, connected devices and network faults in your area. Values shown in red need attention. When all rows are done, tap See coverage in AR.

7 · See coverage in AR

A coverage map is laid over your camera view. Switch between Current and Recommended to compare. The orange dot is your router; the dashed ring is a better spot.

Strong Moderate Weak or dead zone

8 · Explore your home in 3D or VR

Drag to look around; scroll or pinch to zoom. Compare Current, Move router and Add extender to see how much weak area each option removes. Pillars mark the rooms that were actually measured. On a VR headset browser, tap Enter VR; on other devices you stay in 3D mode.

9 · Read your AI diagnosis

The diagnosis checks four places and marks each one OK, a warning or Problem: Network, Router, Room coverage and Your devices. Below it you’ll see the evidence behind the conclusion and what to do next. Tap See full assessment for more detail.

10 · Check the network impact

This screen separates problems inside your home from problems outside it. It shows the impact level outside your home (None, Low or High), the cause, the affected services and customers, an estimated fix time, the recommended actions and whether the case has been escalated.

11 · Follow the guided router check

Point your camera at the router. Once it’s recognised, an orange outline shows what to look at in each check:

  1. Confirm it’s your router.
  2. Power light — should be solid white.
  3. WAN cable — clicked firmly into the orange WAN port.
  4. Internet light — tell the app its colour.
  5. Restart — press the power button once; the app watches the lights for you.

12 · See your result

If the problem is fixed, you’ll see your re-test results. If it isn’t, or the fault is on our network, a support case opens automatically with your line test, router logs, room map and the checks you’ve completed attached, so you won’t need to repeat anything.

13 · Your treatment report

Tap View treatment report to see the before-and-after comparison: signal strength, coverage quality, internet speed and latency, the issues found, the actions taken and your overall improvement. If the fix is still pending, the "after" values are marked ESTIMATED. Tap Share report to send or copy a summary.

Ask Ava, your AI assistant

Tap Ask Ava on any screen. Ava understands your problem, asks a few questions and opens the right camera step for you.

  • Type your problem, or tap a quick reply such as "Wi‑Fi is slow in some rooms".
  • Talk — tap the microphone and speak in English or Arabic.
  • Listen — turn on Voice replies to hear Ava’s answers.
  • Help with this step — available while scanning, in AR, in 3D and during the router check.
  • Move the button — drag Ask Ava anywhere on screen; it stays where you leave it.

If something doesn’t work

ProblemWhat to do
Camera won’t startAllow camera access in your browser settings and reload, or continue with the demo room.
3D view is blankCheck your connection and update your browser. 3D needs WebGL.
"Enter VR" says not supportedVR needs a headset browser. Standard 3D mode works on every phone.
Microphone doesn’t respondSome browsers don’t support voice input. Type to Ava instead.
Number not foundUse the mobile number registered on your broadband account, with 11 digits.
Still not fixedTap Contact support. Your full report is sent with the case.

Glossary

dBmWi‑Fi signal strength. Closer to zero is stronger: −50 is strong, −70 is weak.
MbpsInternet speed, in megabits per second.
LatencyHow long data takes to respond, in milliseconds (ms). Lower is better.
Packet lossThe share of data that never arrives. It should be 0%.
2.4 / 5 GHzThe two Wi‑Fi bands. 5 GHz is faster; 2.4 GHz reaches further but is slower.
WAN portThe router socket that connects to the line from the wall.
ExtenderA small device that repeats Wi‑Fi into rooms the router can’t reach.
